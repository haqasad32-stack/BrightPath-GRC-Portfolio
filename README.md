# BrightPath Retail Group | GRC Portfolio Project

**Cybersecurity Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC)**

## About This Project

I created this project to practice the type of work a Governance, Risk, and Compliance (GRC) analyst does. I wanted to get hands on experience identifying cybersecurity risks, reviewing security controls, documenting weaknesses, and recommending ways to reduce risk.

For this project, I created a fictional retail company called BrightPath Retail Group. I am using it as a case study to work through common cybersecurity and compliance scenarios and learn how to document them in a clear, organized way.

My goal is to build practical skills that are useful for entry level GRC, compliance, cybersecurity risk, and vendor risk roles.

> ***Important Disclaimer:** BrightPath Retail Group is a completely fictional company created for educational and portfolio purposes. This is a simulated case study, not a real security assessment, audit, or compliance review. It does not use or evaluate confidential information from Walmart or any other real organization. Company details, risk scenarios, control descriptions, and assessment results are hypothetical unless clearly stated otherwise.*

## What I Want to Learn

Through this project, I am working on how to:

- Identify cybersecurity risks and explain how they could affect a business.
- Score risks based on likelihood and impact.
- Identify weaknesses in existing security controls.
- Recommend realistic ways to reduce risk.
- Assign risk owners and document follow-up actions.
- Map risks to relevant parts of the NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0.
- Compared selected security practices against ISO/IEC 27001 requirements and controls.
- Review security policies and compare current practices against selected security requirements.
- Write clear findings and recommendations that a business team can understand.

## About the Fictional Company

BrightPath Retail Group is a hypothetical retail business with approximately 500 employees, 10 stores, one corporate office, and one distribution center.

For this case study, the company uses:

- Customer databases and customer information
- An e-commerce website and mobile app
- Point-of-sale (POS) and payment systems
- Microsoft 365 and employee accounts
- Employee laptops and business computers
- Store networks and inventory systems
- Cloud services and third-party vendors

These details provide a realistic setting for practicing cybersecurity risk assessments. They are fictional assumptions, not verified facts about a real company.

## Scope of the Assessment

I am focusing on cybersecurity risks involving customer information, employee accounts, business devices, retail systems, backup and recovery processes, cloud services, and third-party vendor access.

Physical building security, employee performance, product quality, and unrelated business operations are outside the scope of this project.

## How I Score Risks
I use a simple scoring method to estimate the level of risk.

**Risk Score = Likelihood × Impact**

### Likelihood

| Score | Rating         | Meaning                                  |
| ----- | -------------- | ---------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Rare           | Very unlikely to happen                  |
| 2     | Unlikely       | Not expected, but possible               |
| 3     | Possible       | Could happen                             |
| 4     | Likely         | Expected to happen in many circumstances |
| 5     | Almost Certain | Expected to happen frequently            |

### Impact

| Score | Rating     | Meaning                                       |
| ----- | ---------- | --------------------------------------------- |
| 1     | Negligible | Minimal effect on the business                |
| 2     | Minor      | Limited disruption or loss                    |
| 3     | Moderate   | Noticeable business disruption                |
| 4     | Major      | Significant disruption to business operations |
| 5     | Severe     | Severe disruption or major consequences       |

### Risk Rating

| Score | Rating   |
| ----- | -------- |
| 1–5   | Low      |
| 6–10  | Medium   |
| 11–15 | High     |
| 16–25 | Critical |

I also estimate residual risk, which is the risk expected to remain after recommended controls are implemented effectively. These estimates are not verified results. In a real assessment, I would need supporting evidence to confirm whether the controls are working and whether the risk rating should change.

## Frameworks I Am Learning

**NIST Cybersecurity Framework (CSF) 2.0**

I use NIST CSF 2.0 to connect selected risks to relevant cybersecurity outcomes. This helps me understand how individual risks relate to broader areas such as protecting systems, controlling access, and recovering after an incident.

**ISO/IEC 27001**

I plan to use selected ISO 27001 requirements and controls to practice identifying gaps between expected security practices and the fictional company's assumed practices.

Using these frameworks in this project does not mean BrightPath is compliant or certified. The goal is to learn how to apply the frameworks accurately.
